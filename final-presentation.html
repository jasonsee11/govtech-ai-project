Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are a trusted AI assistant supporting ServiceSG officers in responding to residents' enquiries across contact centres, front desks and helpdesks.
Your mission is to help officers deliver accurate, consistent, empathetic and timely responses, while improving first-contact resolution and maintaining public trust.
You are a policy-grounded response assistant, not an autonomous decision-maker. You support officers by interpreting enquiries, retrieving verified policy information, drafting responses and identifying cases that require human judgement.

Task

For each enquiry, follow this workflow:
Step 1: Understand the enquiry
Identify the resident's main question, underlying need and desired outcome.
Review relevant conversation history and attachments, including images or screenshots where supported.
Identify missing information, urgency, complaints or indicators of vulnerability.
Ask only essential clarifying questions. Do not request information already provided.
Step 2: Retrieve and verify information
Search approved government knowledge sources and relevant agency guidance.
Identify the authoritative policy version applicable to the enquiry and its relevant date.
Verify eligibility criteria, procedures, exceptions and service commitments.
Cite the source, relevant provision and effective date where available.
Identify contradictions, outdated information or gaps. Never invent or assume policy details.
If approved sources are unavailable, explicitly state that verification could not be completed.
Step 3: Assess risk and determine handling
Classify the enquiry according to its risk and complexity:
Level
Examples
Required handling
Low
General information, operating hours, service locations
Draft using verified information
Medium
Eligibility, applications, document requirements, policy interpretation
Verify relevant criteria and exceptions; flag ambiguity
High
Sensitive complaints, vulnerable residents, disputed decisions, consequential personal-data matters
Flag for specialist handling and human judgement
When multiple risk indicators exist, apply the highest relevant risk level.
For high-risk cases, prepare a factual summary and escalation recommendation. Draft a substantive reply only when supported by sufficient verified information and appropriate officer instructions.
Step 4: Draft a resident-centred response
Answer the resident's main question directly where the evidence supports an answer.
Use plain, accessible English and a respectful, empathetic tone.
Explain relevant requirements and actionable next steps.
Anticipate likely follow-up questions where reliable information is available.
Avoid unnecessary jargon, lengthy policy quotations or generic redirection.
Do not promise outcomes, approvals or timelines that have not been verified.
Aim to help residents resolve their needs without having to contact ServiceSG repeatedly.
Step 5: Verify and recommend next action
Before presenting the draft:
Check that material factual and policy claims are supported by authoritative sources.
Check that no relevant exceptions, conditions or caveats have been omitted.
Check for contradictions, unsupported assumptions and inappropriate disclosures of personal data.
Classify the evidence as Verified, Partially Verified or Unverified.
Recommend officer review, policy clarification or specialist escalation as appropriate.
If the answer cannot be reliably established, do not manufacture a complete response. State what remains uncertain and what must happen next.

What it demonstrates

Problem: Teachers understand the revised biology curriculum but struggle to translate it into practical assessments within their existing workload. They spend too much time searching, adapting, and verifying questions.

Riskiest assumption: Teachers will trust AI-generated questions enough to use them rather than starting from scratch.

---

Debrief

Treat this as a throwaway prototype.

The prototype exists to test a hypothesis about teacher trust, not to build production code. It has done its job if we learn whether teachers will trust AI-generated questions.

What to carry forward:
- The Keep/Edit/Reject decision flow
- Verification badges with three states
- Expandable details for transparency
- Per-question regeneration (not full-assessment regeneration)
- Failure recovery with multiple options
- Metrics capture approach (retention rate, confidence survey)

What to rebuild:
- Everything else

Known fragilities:
1. State resets on screen transition — `decisions` object clears when navigating back to Screen 1
2. Timer never clears — `setInterval` runs forever, no `clearInterval` on unmount
3. No validation — no checks for empty selections or edge cases

Next engineering phase:
1. Rebuild as a proper application (React/Next.js or similar)
2. Connect to curriculum knowledge base (RAG over MOE syllabus docs)
3. Integrate AI generation with guardrails from `context-skill.md`
4. Add persistence layer (database for decisions, progress)
5. Build test suite for trust scenarios

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: Lower secondary science teachers preparing for the revised biology curriculum.

Problem: Teachers spend an average of 5.5 hours creating one curriculum-aligned biology assessment. They struggle to:
- Interpret curriculum learning outcomes and content boundaries
- Design questions with appropriate difficulty and cognitive demand
- Develop answers and marking schemes
- Check scientific accuracy and curriculum alignment

Target: Under 2 hours per assessment.

Agent bet: Generate curriculum-aligned biology assessment questions with answers and marking schemes by retrieving from the knowledge base and matching teacher requirements.

---

Permissions

Every action the agent takes, with tier and approved setting.

Search curriculum knowledge base: Read, Auto, No side effects
Retrieve marking scheme guidelines: Read, Auto, No side effects
Search sample assessment bank: Read, Auto, No side effects
Generate question text: Draft, Auto, Teacher reviews before use
Generate suggested answer: Draft, Auto, Teacher reviews before use
Generate marking scheme: Draft, Auto, Teacher reviews before use
Assign verification status: Draft, Auto, Teacher sees status on review
Flag question as uncertain: Draft, Auto, Flags are visible, not hidden
Regenerate a single question: Draft, Confirm, Replaces previous draft
Edit question text: Write, Confirm, Modifies teacher's working copy
Edit suggested answer: Write, Confirm, Modifies teacher's working copy
Edit marking scheme: Write, Confirm, Modifies teacher's working copy
Mark question as Keep: Write, Confirm, Records teacher decision
Mark question as Reject: Write, Confirm, Records teacher decision
Save assessment to database: Write, Confirm, Persists work
Export assessment to Word/PDF: Send, Confirm, Leaves the system
Share assessment with other teachers: Send, Block, Reaches other users
Send assessment to students: Send, Block, Reaches students
Log session metrics: Write, Auto, For analytics, no PII
Access teacher profile: Read, Auto, For personalisation
Access student data: Read, Block, Out of scope
Modify knowledge base: Write, Block, Curriculum owner only

Tier definitions:
- Read — retrieves data, no side effects
- Draft — creates content teacher will review before use
- Write — modifies persisted state
- Send — content leaves the system or reaches other people

Setting definitions:
- Auto — agent proceeds without asking
- Confirm — agent proposes, waits for teacher approval
- Block — agent cannot perform this action

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

The agent takes a teacher's selection (topic, learning outcomes, mark allocation) and generates draft assessment questions. For each question, it:

1. Searches the curriculum knowledge base for the selected learning outcome
2. Retrieves mark allocation guidelines for the question type
3. Generates question text appropriate for lower secondary level
4. Generates a suggested answer
5. Generates a marking scheme with point allocation
6. Verifies alignment with syllabus scope
7. Flags uncertainties for teacher review

The agent does not decide what goes into the final assessment. It produces drafts; the teacher decides.

---

Rules of engagement

Cut assessment creation from 5.5 hours to under 2 hours: High, Medium, Knowledge base quality, teacher trust

Free teacher time for actual teaching: High, Medium, Workflow change acceptance

Usage metric

Approved

Primary: Weekly active teachers (WAT) — teachers who generate at least one assessment per week.

Pilot (Week 1–4): 10–20 teachers, Approved
Scale (Week 5–12): 100+ teachers, Approved
Steady state: 500+ teachers, Approved

Why this metric: If teachers aren't using the agent voluntarily, the value proposition has failed — regardless of eval scores.

---

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Approved

Watch: Silent subtle errors (Dimension 1 failures) in the first 2 weeks of pilot.

0 silent errors in Week 1–2: Guardrails working, Continue pilot
1–2 silent errors in Week 1–2: Edge cases exist, Add to test cases, patch, re-eval
3+ silent errors in Week 1–2: Systematic gap, Pause pilot, root cause analysis

Why this pattern: Silent subtle errors are the deal-breaker type that erodes trust. If they appear early, the agent isn't ready.

---

Stop line

Approved

Any deal-breaker case fails in eval: Block release, Engineering Lead
<50% question retention in pilot: Stop pilot, rework, Product Owner
<80% error detection in pilot: Stop pilot, rework, Product Owner
3+ silent subtle errors in production: Suspend agent, incident review, Product Owner
Teacher reports content used with students that contained undetected error: Suspend agent, immediate review, System Admin → Product Owner

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was translating a promising AI use case into a reliable product. Generating assessment questions was relatively easy; defining the guardrails, handling failures and ensuring teachers could trust the output required much more thought.

Key learnings

Start with the problem and riskiest assumption, not the technology. Build small, test early and measure real outcomes rather than just whether the AI works. Keep humans in control, make uncertainty visible and establish clear conditions for when to stop.

Aha moment

My biggest shift was learning to work backwards from the desired outcomes and evaluations, rather than starting with what AI can do. Trust is especially critical in MOE. By defining teacher personas, deal-breakers and clear evaluation thresholds upfront, we can work backwards to design and optimise the AI product for what truly matters. Evals should shape what we build, not just test what we've built.
